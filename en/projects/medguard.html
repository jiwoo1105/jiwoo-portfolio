MedGuard

Comparing how adversarial inputs affect classification and purification.

The problemApproachReported experiments

The problem

When input perturbations change a prediction.

I compared adversarial attacks and defenses for a binary pneumonia classifier on chest X-rays. The experiments examined frequency differences between pixel-space and VAE latent-space attacks, and the effects of purification on classification and image structure.

I connected a ResNet-50 classifier, FGSM/PGD and a DiffAttack-style implementation, FFT visualizations, and a Gradio comparison demo. I used AI coding tools for implementation and debugging and organized the experimental conditions and results.

Approach

Comparing perturbation patterns and purification strength.

I used FFT analysis to inspect changes in high-frequency components and experimented with purification using the RoentGen VAE and blending the purified output with the input image. The repository’s Medical DiffPure is an experimental pipeline that considers attack-specific purification strength and structural consistency in lung regions.

A small frequency change cannot reliably distinguish a clean image from every attack. I therefore treat the demo’s attack-classification rules as part of a controlled experiment, not as a general attack-detection guarantee.

Reported experiments

Comparing defenses within the reported setup.

The public repository reports robust accuracy of 58.31% under FGSM, 61.47% under PGD, and 78.56% under DiffAttack for Medical DiffPure. The DiffAttack result is 4.84 percentage points higher than the reported 73.72% for Vanilla DiffPure.

These are results recorded for the repository’s experimental setup. They do not establish superiority across medical-image models or unseen attacks. Adaptive attacks, other data distributions, and preservation of diagnostic features after purification require further evaluation.
