Project overview

Removing an attack can also remove information needed for classification.

Defending a chest X-ray classifier was not simply a matter of removing more noise. Purification changes both the classification input and its structure. I connected a ResNet-50 pneumonia classifier, FGSM, PGD and DiffAttack-style attacks, FFT analysis and purification comparisons.

Core challenge · Defense and information preservation

How can attacks be reduced while preserving information needed for classification?

The challenge
Purification changes the original image as well as the perturbation. Its effect varies by attack, so stronger purification alone cannot establish both classification performance and information preservation.
Why this approach
I compared frequency changes, VAE purification and input blending across attack conditions, examining classification results under matched settings alongside image structure.

What I did

I inspected frequency changes with FFT and varied RoentGen VAE purification and blending with the input. The experimental path considers attack conditions and lung-region structure alongside the purification result.

Small frequency changes do not reliably distinguish a clean image from a latent-space attack. The demo’s routing rule is therefore an experimental heuristic, not a guarantee of attack detection.

Reporting the result together with its comparison conditions.

Under the repository’s DiffAttack setup, Medical DiffPure achieved 78.56% accuracy versus 73.72% for Vanilla DiffPure, a 4.84 percentage-point difference. Medical DiffPure recorded 58.31% and 61.47% under FGSM and PGD, respectively.

These are comparisons under the recorded settings. They do not validate unseen attacks, other distributions, preservation of pathology after purification or clinical outcomes.

What I learned

I learned to distinguish post-defense classification scores from image preservation, checking whether stronger transformations also changed useful information.

Results & learning

Separating accuracy recovery from information preservation.

Stating the attack conditions and preserving comparable results was essential to interpretation. I learned to evaluate a defense by what it changes and preserves as well as by its average score.
