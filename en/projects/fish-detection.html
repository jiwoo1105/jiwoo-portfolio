Project overview

From a tank image to a result tied to each fish.

Ripples, lighting and overlapping fish make it difficult to associate symptoms with an individual in a full-frame image. I separated detection, cropping and seven-class visual-symptom classification, then connected predictions back to their positions in the app.

My work covered label preprocessing, detection and classification training, the inference API, coordinate conversion and Flutter results UI. Disease and response guidance uses symptom-based rules, not image-based confirmation of a pathogen.

Demo inputs include AI-generated footage. They do not demonstrate field validation or early-detection outcomes at a working farm.

Core challenge · Reliable per-fish analysis

How can the same fish be preserved across detection, classification and display?

The challenge
Ripples, lighting and overlap can cause missed detections. Strong crop-classification scores cannot recover fish missed upstream, and the app must still align each prediction with its source position.
Why this approach
I split the workflow into detection, per-fish crops, symptom classification and source-coordinate display, checking labels, training distribution, stage metrics and coordinate transforms separately.

What I did

I mapped AI Hub object and symptom annotations to crops and excluded unmapped labels. Class caps were applied only to training data, leaving the validation distribution unchanged.

Peak validation Top-1 classification accuracy was 87.68%, while detection mAP50 was 0.4455. I kept the metrics separate and identified missed fish in overlapping or turbid scenes as an upstream bottleneck.

Making sure a prediction and its overlay refer to the same fish.

I converted model coordinates to source-image and app-display coordinates and passed position, symptom and confidence together. FastAPI responses were connected to Flutter so users could inspect individual predictions and detection history by tank.

What I learned

I learned not to judge a pipeline by its strongest model score. An upstream miss can remove the input that a later stage needs.

Results & learning

Preserving the same object from preprocessing to the interface.

Explaining a result required the label meaning, detected object, crop classification and display coordinates to stay aligned. I learned to separate training-data changes from validation and inspect both stage-level bottlenecks and final overlays.
