The problem

Answering the question fully and returning a complete response.

We built a medical question-answering system around the L2-preview model provided for the hackathon. The challenge was to address the user’s requests, include relevant evidence and cautions, and return a complete response within a limited inference budget.

The work focused on processing around model calls rather than training model weights. I contributed to MCP retrieval and generation, a medication safety gate, checks for unsupported citations and repeated searches, and multi-turn response improvements.

Evidence retrieval

Matching retrieval to the question.

The initial pipeline connected classification, retrieval, and generation. The team later adopted direct generation as the default, with selective MCP lookups for questions requiring official information such as medication prices, approvals, indications, and medical codes.

More retrieval was not treated as an improvement by itself. I reviewed failed lookups, ambiguous references, and duplicate searches, and refined the flow to avoid adding figures or citations unsupported by the retrieved evidence.

Inference design

Managing context, time, and output length together.

I worked on strengthening the response instructions for the latest user message while preserving conversation history. Experiments encouraged requests for relevant test results or medication lists and addressed responses that covered only part of a question or stopped at a referral.

The team’s current system includes token and time budgets, retries for truncated or empty outputs, and additional review only when defects are suspected. I distinguish the changes in my own commits from the final system developed by the team.

Team outcome

First place in the benchmark, awarded to Team AIM.

Team AIM received the benchmark award for the highest score in the HealthBench-based evaluation at Lunit’s Conquer Health hackathon. The certificate below documents the team award.

The team’s public final evaluation record reports an average of 46.51 across three rounds and first place among 14 teams in the final evaluation. The certificate does not state a numerical score, so the award evidence and the team-reported scores are identified separately.

Lessons and limits

Each change needs its own evaluation.

Development scores and observations about individual prompts are kept separate from the final evaluation record. The score contribution of the multi-turn instruction change was not isolated. The project taught me to design for response completeness and evaluation conditions as well as tool integration.

These are hackathon benchmark results, not clinical validation on patients.
