Conquer Health

I contributed to Team AIM’s HealthBench benchmark award by implementing and reviewing evidence retrieval and inference workflows for medical dialogue.

Context & my role

Making retrieved documents useful to the answer.

The task was to improve medical question answering without changing the provided L2-preview weights. A successful lookup was insufficient if the relevant document text did not reach generation or the answer missed part of the question.

I implemented the initial MCP retrieval/generation connection and medication safety gate, checking evidence delivery separately from whether the response addressed the question and used that evidence. I also worked on output-budget experiments and multi-turn response instructions. Individual contributions and the final team submission are distinguished by commit.

Baseline & decisions

Testing direct generation before adding more stages.

The team ledger records 29.13 for a retrieval/routing candidate and 32.44 for direct L2 generation. The team used direct generation as the baseline and examined temperature=0, a short instruction to address the user’s requests, and recovery that regenerates without thinking when a thinking-enabled response is truncated or empty.

Repeated evaluations of the same serving behavior with thinking and failure recovery yielded 49.72, 50.03, and 47.79. Across the broader development history, model-serving conditions and cumulative changes also varied, so score differences between distinct candidates were not treated as isolated effects of individual techniques.

My experiment

A larger output budget did not establish a score improvement.

After rechecking the output limit, my candidate e6de327 corrected the configured server cap from 2,048 to 32,768 and raised the requested output budget from 2,048 to 4,096 tokens. The goal was to reduce truncation that triggered regeneration. The server cap and requested output budget are distinct settings.

The ledger records 49.59 for this candidate, 0.41 points above the baseline’s three-run mean of 49.18. The difference lies within the observed run-to-run variation, so it does not establish a benefit from increasing the token budget alone. Truncation, fallback frequency, and latency also needed inspection.

Final approach

Keeping direct generation and retrieving only where an exact lookup was needed.

The team’s later submission sequence restored the established baseline, added selective MCP lookup, resolved references in follow-up questions, and preserved longer answers. Retrieval was limited to explicit Korean queries such as medication prices, approvals, ingredients, and disease codes. References such as “that drug” or “that code” were resolved only when recent conversation identified a unique target.

Failed or ambiguous lookups preserved the direct-generation path. The post-processing character limit was also raised from 6,000 to 12,000 to retain complete answers. This final sequence was team work; assessing selective MCP’s score contribution requires logs showing where it actually ran.

Results & evidence

First place in the benchmark, with evaluation stages kept separate.

Team AIM won Lunit’s Conquer Health benchmark award for the highest HealthBench-based evaluation score. The team’s public final record reports a three-round average of 46.51 and first place among 14 teams. The certificate below documents the award but does not state a numerical score.

The development ledger reviewed here records 54.37 for ef3609d and 53.53 with first place on the closing dashboard. These are separate from the final three-round evaluation. The mapping of the closing score to Trial 56 remains an inference, not a confirmed submission result.

What I learned

A promising score still needs a trustworthy comparison.

Scores for the same behavior ranged from 47.79 to 50.03, a 2.24-point spread. The team used a ±2.5-point empirical threshold to avoid accepting small changes based on a single submission. This was an operating rule based on three observations, not a statistically established confidence interval.

Tool-call success, evidence use, response completeness, and benchmark score were distinct things to check. The experience taught me to explain what changed under which conditions and retain inconclusive experiments as evidence for the next decision.

These are hackathon experiments and award records, not clinical validation on patients.
